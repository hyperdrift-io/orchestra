# ai.hyperdrift.io — the design concept (before any screen)

14 September 2026. Rounds 1 and 2 rejected by the founder: "they're all really bad. We need to generate the ideal holistic design before ScreenCraft gets involved." The failure was the method. Both rounds locked a nine-item page skeleton and asked Recraft to render it; Recraft returned the median agency template nine times per round. This document is the missing step: the ideal, uncompromised design as an idea, before Recraft photographs anything that looks like a screen and before ScreenCraft measures anything.

Order of work from here: **concept in words → concept images (Recraft, the idea of the whole site, uncompromised, not restricted by what code can do) → founder reviews the concept images and refines the idea → only then ScreenCraft maps the chosen concept → implementation.** Founder rule, 14 September: concepts are generated from images; we are creating an idea, not code. Recraft never renders navigation, buttons, forms or copy; it renders the world and the idea.

## What this site is, in one line each

- **The promise:** AI inside your product is the fast track to growth. A clear path, and it leads to profit.
- **The buyer:** a technical founder or CTO of a working B2B SaaS. Their product earns; the roadmap cannot pause; nothing may break tenancy, audit or cost.
- **The relationship:** founder to founder across a desk. Hyperdrift runs its own fleet, builds in public, and has already fitted AI inside products that run.
- **The stance:** the old way is decks, discovery theatre and a new framework every quarter. The new way is agents shipped inside the live product in weeks, with people in control.
- **The proof:** Standup, Helm, uk.gov Radar, unanswered, Deputy MCP. Built for AWS, Google, OpenAI and DEV challenges. Dated, public, inspectable. Never an award, never a metric we did not measure.
- **The door:** the visitor says where they are (first step, workflow, prototype, live product) and sees the work and the next step for that place. Approved interaction, kept.
- **The voice:** bold, plain, human. Speak to Enable. No hype, no shame, no invented numbers.

The feeling at the moment the site works: *"They have done this. They know where I am. I can see my next step."*

## Content the design must carry

The hero statement and the question. Four situations, each with one response, one action and two inspectable examples. The proof, dated, with organiser credit. Intel's latest edition and the latest article, dated from the feeds. The anniversary. The Traction Partnership. One enquiry with "where you are" prefilled. Everything else folds.

## Three concepts, three different answers to "how does the fast track feel?"

Each is a whole world, not a palette. Each names its signature, how the surfaces behave inside it, the one motion, exactly what Recraft supplies, and how it fails.

### 1. The Engine, running — growth feels like power fitted into a machine that never stopped

**Signature:** the whole site is one engine on a test stand, running, and Hyperdrift is fitting the new part while it runs. One work lamp, one camera angle, dark workshop. The flywheel never stops turning; that is your product, still earning while the AI goes in.

- **First screen:** the engine, close, lamp-lit, the new bright component half seated, the wheel blurred with motion. The statement set large in the dark above it. The question beneath. Nothing else.
- **The door:** four stations along the engine, left to right, the way an engineer walks it: intake (finding a first step), drive (automating a workflow), test bench (taking a prototype live), tuning (improving a live product). Choosing a station moves the lamp. The station's response, action and two examples appear in the lit circle; the rest of the floor stays dark.
- **The proof:** the parts wall behind the stand. Each shipped part hangs tagged: name, what it was built for, the date, SHIPPED or IN DEVELOPMENT. Read from the catalogue, never typed.
- **Now playing and the anniversary:** the shift log on the bench, dated in the lamp light.
- **Partnership:** a second stand in the dark, empty, with the lamp off. "No budget? Then we partner." Turning it on is the enquiry.
- **Enquiry:** the job card clipped to the stand. Where you are is already ticked.
- **Type:** one industrial grotesk with real width range, two sizes; tabular figures for dates. Warm white on near-black, one lamp colour.
- **Motion:** the wheel turns, slowly, forever. The lamp moves when you choose a station. Reduced motion: the wheel is a still with blur baked into the photograph; the lamp cuts, no travel.
- **Recraft supplies:** the engine on its stand under the lamp, one angle, three states (before, fitting, running), and the tagged parts wall. See `round3-concept/01-the-engine.png`.
- **Risk:** it can read as heavy industry, not software, and a CTO may not see their SaaS in a flywheel. Mitigation: the "engine" is a precise, small, beautiful machine, not a factory; the parts on the wall are the real products, named.

### 2. The Line — growth feels like one line you can follow to the end

**Signature:** the whole site is one line, drawn in real ink by a plotter on heavy paper, rising from the first screen to the enquiry. It is the growth curve and the path to profit at once. Everything on the site hangs off that line; nothing sits beside it.

- **First screen:** paper. The line begins bottom-left and rises. The statement is set on the paper above the line; the question sits where the line first turns upward. The ink is still wet at the leading end.
- **The door:** four marks on the line, in order, each a short tick with its label. The visitor's mark is bold. Choosing a mark pulls the response, action and two examples out along a short branch line drawn from that point. Only one branch is drawn at a time.
- **The proof:** the plotter's log along the bottom edge of the paper: what was drawn, for whom, when, in plotter monospace. SHIPPED is a solid tick; IN DEVELOPMENT is a dashed one.
- **Now playing and the anniversary:** three small marginal notes in the plotter's hand, dated.
- **Partnership:** the line has a second, fainter line beneath it that joins the main line later: the partnership. "We draw it together; you keep the paper."
- **Enquiry:** the end of the line. The pen lifts; the visitor writes the next point.
- **Type:** one monospace for everything the plotter "wrote", one grotesk for the statement. Ink on cream. One signal colour for the wet ink only.
- **Motion:** the line draws itself once on first visit, in about two seconds, then never again. Reduced motion: the line is already drawn.
- **Recraft supplies:** macro photographs of a pen plotter drawing a rising ink line on heavy paper: the ink, the bleed, the tooth of the paper, the pen. Pigment and material studies only. See `round3-concept/02-the-line.png`.
- **Risk:** austerity can read as a wireframe, and a line is the easiest thing in the world to make generic. The concept lives or dies on the ink being real: bleed, tooth, the wet leading edge. If the board reads as a vector diagram, the concept has failed honestly.

### 3. The Split — growth feels like walking out of one room into another

**Signature:** every screen is one photograph cut down the middle. Left: the old way, a beige meeting room, a deck on the projector, chairs empty. Right: the same room stripped to a bench where the product runs with an agent inside it, dawn coming through the window. The cut is the site's only grid line. The visitor is always standing on the cut.

- **First screen:** the cut, full bleed. The statement runs across it, the words "the old way" struck through on the left of the cut in the visitor's own reading. The question sits on the right, in the light.
- **The door:** four cuts, stacked. Each situation is its own before/after: the first step (a whiteboard of ideas / one bounded use case running), the workflow (a hand-run spreadsheet / an agent doing it with a person confirming), the prototype (a demo on a laptop / the same thing inside the product behind a flag), the live product (a dashboard nobody reads / the signal that mattered, acted on). Choosing one widens its right half; the response, action and examples live in the light.
- **The proof:** on the right of every cut, the real things that were shipped are in the room: Standup on a screen, Helm's crew, Radar's map. Tagged with what they were built for and when.
- **Now playing and the anniversary:** on the right, on the bench, dated.
- **Partnership:** one more cut, the left half empty: "No budget?" The right half is the bench already set up.
- **Enquiry:** the door on the right of the last cut. Where you are is which cut you came from.
- **Type:** one grotesk; heavy on the right, light and struck through on the left. Two grounds: beige-grey left, dawn-white right. One signal colour for the cut and for actions.
- **Motion:** the cut moves. Hover or focus on any split slides the cut left, giving the new way more room; choosing commits it. Reduced motion: the cut sits at 40/60 and does not travel.
- **Recraft supplies:** the two rooms. One continuous scene photographed twice from the same camera: the meeting room with the deck, and the same room as the bench at dawn. Then the four paired subjects. See `round3-concept/03-the-split.png`.
- **Risk:** before/after is an advertising trope and can become a cheap shot at the reader's own meeting room. The Voice Covenant forbids shame. The left half must be dignified and a little sad, never mocked, and the right half must show real work, not a fantasy. If the board reads as a mattress ad, the concept has failed.

## Recommendation

**The Line.** It is the founder's sentence made literal, "a clear path that leads to profit", it makes the approved stage entry native (the visitor marks where they are on the line), it is the cheapest to build and the most honest for a CTO buyer, and it carries "proven" as a plotter log rather than a boast. Its only risk is the one a designer can control. The Engine is the most physical and the best at "AI inside your product while it runs", and should be the board that argues with the founder's taste for the visceral. The Split is the loudest expression of "disrupting the old way" and the riskiest under the Voice Covenant.

## What happens next, in order

1. Founder reviews the concept images in `round3-concept/`: for each world, the world itself (`0N-*.png`) and two holistic concept images of the whole site as an idea (`0N-concept-*.png`). Founder picks a world, asks for the idea to be pushed further in images, or rejects it. No code, no boards, no ScreenCraft until the founder says the concept is right.
2. The chosen concept is refined in images until it is the ideal, uncompromised design.
3. `SELECTION.md` records the pick. Only then `hd screencraft analyze` maps the chosen concept image, and implementation follows on a new branch from `stage-entry-b-2026-09-14`, keeping the data sources and the enquiry route.

## What was wrong in rounds 1 and 2, so it does not recur

- A nine-item copy skeleton, a desktop-left mobile-right layout and three metaphors were handed to an image model. Every degree of freedom where a concept could live was removed for comparability; what came back was the same agency template in three coats.
- Recraft was used as a UI renderer. It is a photographer. Its job is the world.
- The "directions" were metaphors for a layout (a desk, a map, a console, a track, a ledger, a manifesto), not worlds with a signature that every surface obeys.
- Nothing in either round said what the visitor feels at the moment the site works. That sentence now leads this document.

# Round 4 — the giant

Founder, 14 September, after rejecting all three round-3 worlds: **"User should feel they will stand on the shoulder of a giant that will understand their business and drive it to prosperity."**

That sentence is the concept. Everything below is three ways to make it a place. The giant is Hyperdrift: enormous capability, and attentive, the kind that bends down to understand before it lifts. The founder is small and safe and can see further than they ever could. Prosperity is a lit horizon, a harvest, a city waking up. Nothing menacing, nothing sci-fi, nothing corporate.

Two sentences of copy in the images: "Stand on the shoulders of a giant who understands your business." and "Where are you taking AI next?"

### 4A — On the shoulder: the giant made visible
A monumental, calm figure, sculptural and human, walking at dawn through a golden landscape. A founder stands on its shoulder and points at the horizon; the giant looks where the founder points. That is "understands your business": it goes where you point. Prosperity is the sunlit valley ahead, fields and a city waking. Warm gold, long shadows, the founder tiny but the sharpest thing in the frame. Risk: fantasy-poster cliché; the giant must be sculpture and weather, not a character.

### 4B — The view: the giant implied
No figure. Only altitude. The founder, small, stands at the edge of an immense warm shoulder-shaped ridge of stone above the morning cloud, looking out. Far below and ahead, their own business: a small lit town spreading into a plain of gold fields and roads under sunrise. The elevation is the giant. Risk: could read as a landscape stock photo; the ridge must be unmistakably a shoulder, the town unmistakably theirs.

### 4C — The lift: the giant as careful hands
Colossal, gentle hands, real skin in cinematic light, raising a small glowing workshop with lit windows and a chimney still working, the founder's product, up into the morning above a field of ripe wheat. Understanding is the care in the hands; prosperity is the harvest below and the light above. Risk: hands are hard for image models and sentiment tips into greeting-card. Keep the light hard and the crop tight.
