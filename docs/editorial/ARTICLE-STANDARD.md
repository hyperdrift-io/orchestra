# Articles that start useful conversations

Founder direction, 22 September 2026. Applies to new articles for `ai.hyperdrift.io/articles`, including the Databricks / AI engineering series. Read `MISSION.md` and `meta/PHILOSOPHY.md` §8 first. This is the AI site's editorial standard; it takes precedence over the legacy blog's audience, format and CTA guidance.

## Outcome and audience

Help a founder, CTO or engineering lead make one consequential decision, then invite a relevant conversation about their own workflow. Qualified enquiries are the business outcome. Views, reading completion, shares and CTA clicks diagnose the path; they are not ROI by themselves. Databricks is a concrete platform through which to teach portable AI engineering concepts, not a mandatory answer or an implied partnership.

## The publication contract

Choose the visual format from the explanation. Use a sequence or state diagram for flows, a boundary map for architecture, and a data visualisation for measured comparisons. Founder correction, 22 September: do not use the `infographic` skill for these architecture/flow articles; it is appropriate to data visualisation. Design purpose-built visuals directly in the portal’s identity. Essential labels must remain readable on a phone; recompose or simplify a dense graphic instead of shrinking the desktop export. Use actual current CSS tokens rather than inheriting the legacy HD blog palette.

- **Short:** aim for 500–800 words of body copy, approximately 3–4 minutes. Exclude captions and source lists from the budget, but do not hide a second article in appendices. Split separate decisions into separate pieces.
- **Useful immediately:** title the actual decision; give the answer and who it fits in the opening. Product names belong in comparison titles and explanations when readers need them. Avoid a generic AI introduction.
- **One simple example:** walk through one recognisable workflow with a concrete input, decision and result. Label invented examples as illustrative. Code is optional; when included, keep it small, state the version and prerequisites, and distinguish pseudocode from something executed.
- **An image is required:** every article has an original, relevant opening image and at least one striking, useful explanatory visual. Place a clearly visible, borderless expertise globe to the right of the title, sharing its row height on viewports wider than 850px. At 850px and below, omit this decorative globe and its control so the headline keeps the available width; do not allocate a hidden WebGL renderer. Do not add a separate hero stripe, background panel or overlap with the words. The founder rejected the faint mesh behind the headline and then moved the stripe beside it to save space. Treat the article as an expertise domain: use a continuously rotating spherical mesh and connected topic forms, with smooth two-axis cursor tilt across the header and a small pause control; stop offscreen, in hidden tabs and under reduced-motion preferences. Do not pulse brightness. Render the topic artwork with WebGL using the homepage’s wireframe object family and a matching SVG fallback inlined into the server HTML for loading, no JavaScript, unavailable WebGL or context loss. Reserve its dimensions, wait for a painted GPU frame before crossfading, and begin motion after that handover. Use a distinct, relevant composition in the portal’s visual language; place the detailed visual within the explanation instead of using a tall chart as a hero. Existing proof media stays separate from editorial art.
- **Compact layout:** the title, adjacent globe and excerpt should lead quickly into the first paragraph. Keep “In this article” always expanded. Highlight the section at the reading position, and update its bookmarkable URL fragment with replaceState so scrolling does not flood browser history. Preserve native anchor links without JavaScript. Keep paragraph spacing restrained and body text comfortably readable. Do not repeat a detailed visual as a second steps panel. Preserve touch targets, source access and the contextual enquiry. Check the actual first-paragraph position and page height on phones, rather than shrinking type to meet a height target.
- **Visual quality:** one decision, legible hierarchy, ink/cream/gold site identity, restrained accent colour, generous space and accurate labels. Use editable SVG or HTML/CSS for factual diagrams, export PNG for sharing, and retain the source. Use restrained interaction when it reveals a meaningful difference, with keyboard support and a useful static state. Include `ai.hyperdrift.io/articles`, a plain-language takeaway and a short source note. Every number needs a source or an explicit illustrative label; no invented benchmarks or unsupported scoring. Check the export and article at desktop and phone widths. Supply meaningful alt text and a text explanation of the decision.
- **Earn credibility:** cite current primary documentation near material claims. Record source/version and research date. Distinguish vendor capabilities, our recommendations, conceptual examples, executed experiments and client results. Never describe a source link or diagram as a deployed implementation. Say when a product feature is beta.
- **A clear recommendation with limits:** state when each approach fits and the condition that would change the recommendation. Compare equivalent layers and workloads; never manufacture a winner from unlike products.
- **One primary invitation:** use a topic-specific CTA such as “Discuss your agent data model”. Explain what to bring (workflow, current stack, constraint) and what the conversation will clarify. Repeat that same action only at natural decision points. Sharing and related reading are secondary; no competing newsletter or generic “learn more” close.
- **Portable takeaway:** one share line of at most 20 words and one practical next step. A timely signal is useful when sourced; do not invent news to satisfy a template.

## Authoring and preview

The current article implementation lives on the `feat-ai-native-articles` worktree; locate it using `git worktree list` from the Orchestra checkout before editing. Read its `AGENTS.md`. Use `content/articles/<slug>.md` and `src/data/articles.json`. The renderer supports paragraphs, second-level headings, quotations, images and inline links/emphasis/code; it does not support MDX components, tables or fenced code. Explain small examples in prose or an image rather than silently shipping unsupported syntax.

Catalogue entries require an image, contextual CTA, honest evidence description and `publishedAt: null` until publication is approved. Define article symbols in `src/data/article-mesh.json` and regenerate SVGs with `node docs/editorial/databricks-series/generate-article-mesh.cjs`. `MeshArtwork` renders the live geometry. Use optional `headerImage` for its shallow fallback artwork and `visualization` for a purpose-built visual, takeaway, text equivalent and sources. `ArticleBody` places the visual after the opening quotation; the real media proof remains available for articles that have it. Keep research, image sources and editorial decisions in `docs/editorial/`. Reuse the existing enquiry attribution and preview delivery mode. Never count local preview submissions as leads. Check the article, its image, its social card and the preselected enquiry context in a running local preview.

Review the final words and images before publication. Actual publication dates only. Publication approval does not follow automatically from a request to draft; do not deploy, submit sitemaps, send mail or post announcements as part of drafting.

## Return on the work

Start with a buyer's decision connected to a service we can credibly deliver. Prefer an answer supported by inspectable work, current documentation and a reusable visual over a longer feature catalogue. Record a search-intent hypothesis, evidence of demand if available, CTA, distribution assets and production effort for each piece. Unknown search volume stays unknown.

Reuse the same idea as an infographic, a short founder post and a short email introduction. Prepare these alongside the draft; publishing or sending still needs authorization. Interlink genuinely related articles and relevant service/proof pages. Preserve one canonical article URL.

Reuse the existing funnel: `article_viewed` → `article_cta_clicked` → `enquiry_started` → server-confirmed `enquiry_submitted` → human qualification. Compare unique sessions within a defined cohort; the engagement marker only records reaching the end. Primary business rate: unique qualified enquiries attributable to the article / unique eligible article sessions. Also record qualified conversations per production hour and eventual attributable gross profit against creation, distribution and maintenance cost. Label assisted attribution and estimates; a click is not a won engagement.

At low volume, use actual enquiries and reader feedback before A/B tests. When sufficient traffic supports an experiment, predefine one hypothesis, primary metric, downstream qualification guardrail and sample requirement. If evidence is sparse, report uncertainty and improve exposure or measurement; do not declare a winner from a date or a handful of clicks.
