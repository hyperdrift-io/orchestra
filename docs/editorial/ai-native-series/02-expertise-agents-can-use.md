# Give your agents the knowledge your best work depends on

Your best work carries knowledge that a new colleague would need time to learn: which evidence matters, what a good result looks like, when to ask for help. An agent needs access to that knowledge too.

At Hyperdrift, we put recurring instructions into skills: written guidance an agent loads for a particular job. The useful part is the judgement recorded inside them.

> Write down what good work requires before asking an agent to do it.

## A real example: publishing an article

Our publishing skill explains who the article serves, where the source belongs, what evidence a claim needs and how to prepare a preview. It points to the shared tone guide rather than maintaining another version of it. It also says the founder reads the final words before publication.

That last instruction makes the division of work explicit. The agent can investigate, draft and assemble the proof. The author remains responsible for what goes out under their name.

One instruction captures the standard: “Read `apps/hyper-drift/TONE.md` before writing or editing any article.” It sounds modest. Its value is that the next assignment starts from the same editorial context.

The [Hyperdrift anniversary](https://hyperdrift.io/blog/hyperdrift-turns-one) provides a public example of the work this practice supports: an article with linked builds, a narrated film and a source ledger. It is a published artefact, not a measurement of how much time the skill saved.

## Make a correction useful twice

When a draft misses the tone, correcting that paragraph helps the article. Correcting the instruction that produced it can help the next article too.

We maintain a shared communication rule: explain what the user can do, and describe missing evidence as a limit of our knowledge. Our Foundation tooling checks whether known agent prompts reference that rule. It can identify a missing reference; it cannot certify that every sentence is thoughtful or clear. Editorial judgement still matters.

Skills need maintenance for the same reason procedures do. A new publishing destination changes links and release instructions. A lesson from a real failure changes the checklist. An instruction that once helped may eventually become clutter.

For this series, that means preserving the editorial standards while moving the intended publication and enquiry destination to ai.hyperdrift.io.

## Start with a job your team knows well

A strong first candidate has examples of accepted work, a recognisable input and someone who can judge the result. Preparing a service handover, reviewing a support response or assembling a project brief can all fit that shape.

Those are potential client applications. The initial work is to learn your team's actual practice: which sources it trusts, what it checks and where responsibility changes hands. A generic prompt cannot supply that knowledge.

Written instructions also cannot grant or enforce system permissions. If an agent must not send a message or change a record, that boundary belongs in its tools and approval flow as well.

**What to watch:** as agents move between tasks and tools, portable instructions can keep specialist practice accessible. Take one accepted piece of work and annotate the decisions that made it good. That is a useful starting point for a skill.

Send this to the colleague whose expertise everyone comes to borrow. We can help turn one recurring job into a workflow your team can review, operate and improve.
